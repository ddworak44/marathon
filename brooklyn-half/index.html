NYCRUNS Brooklyn Experience Half Marathon

A 22-week plan to go from 1–3 mile outings to 13.1 miles in Prospect Park. Train three days a week with an easy run/walk and aim for a finish.

How to start Weekly schedule Race pace and cutoff

Start where you are

If a mile is your usual outing
Use the low end of the ranges. Try 30 seconds of easy jogging and 60–90 seconds of walking. If you currently run once a week, make Thursday a brisk walk for the first two weeks.
If 2–3 miles already feel comfortable
Use the upper end if you’ve been running regularly and recovering well. Try 2 minutes of easy jogging and 1 minute of walking, or jog continuously if you can talk in full sentences.

Start and finish each training outing with a few minutes of walking, included in the listed time or distance. If you’re using intervals, take walk breaks from the start. On a joint run, use a short loop and regroup each lap so both of you can stay at an easy effort.

Adjust week by week. If the long run feels overly hard or you’re still unusually sore by Tuesday, repeat the last comfortable week or shorten the next outing. Skip missed sessions and carry on from a distance you’ve handled. Stop a run if pain changes your stride, and get persistent pain checked.

Half-marathon training and fueling guidance

Plan for the cutoff

NYCRUNS lists a standard half-marathon limit of 3 hours 30 minutes, about 16:00 per mile. The Brooklyn race page lists starts through 9:40 a.m. and an event end time of 12:30 p.m. Ask NYCRUNS how course closures apply to your wave and check the race guide when it’s released. NYCRUNS time limits · Race details

Use elapsed pace from your rehearsals, including walk breaks and stops. If the standard limit applies to your wave, 15:00–15:30 per mile gives you roughly 7–13 minutes of room, provided that pace feels sustainable. Treat the pace examples below as planning estimates.

Decide after the Apr 11 long run

Look for an easy finish to the long run, normal recovery and enough pace margin for your wave. If you’re still well short of the long-run distance or the cutoff pace takes a hard effort for several miles, consider a later race or one with more time on course.

On race day

Start the first two miles at an easy effort and use the walk breaks you practiced. Shorten your stride on the climb toward Prospect Park and stick with familiar fuel. Each of you can run your own pace and meet at a spot you’ve agreed on after the finish.
